# Application answers

## Research before writing a word

Find what this company actually screens for. Recruiter interviews, hiring
guides, founder posts about culture. Specific signals, not "show passion". Then
map the candidate's real experience onto those signals.

## The swap test

Replace the company name with a direct competitor. Does the answer still work?

If yes, it is not an answer, it is a template. Rewrite until it contains
something **only true about this company**, framed through something the
candidate has actually done.

## While writing

- Do not recite the company's achievements back at them. They know. Frame their
  decisions through what the candidate has learned from their own work.
- No Hollywood lines. Write like a smart person typing an important email.
- Run the anti-AI check *during* writing, not after. See the `writing-voice`
  skill. Writing model-shaped prose and sanding it down afterwards leaves the
  shape behind.

## Process

Research first, write once. One draft, not nine. Use subagents for research, not
for producing competing drafts to pick between: that produces an average, and an
average cover letter is a rejected one.
